Recipes privacy policy

Effective date: November 1, 2026

Recipes: Cook, Plan & Share ("Recipes", "the app") is made by Ridgewood Apps (Aaron Beck Brunk). This policy says what the app does with your information. The short version: your recipes stay on your phone unless you choose to share them, there are no ads, and nothing is tracked.

What stays on your phone

Your recipes, photos, meal plan, pantry, grocery list, ratings, swaps and food preferences are stored on your phone. You don't need an account to use Recipes, and without one nothing you add leaves your phone, except in the cases below that you turn on.

Your food preferences (your name in the app, favourite cuisines, spice level and dietary choices) are never shared, even with your household.

Android may include the app's data in your phone's own backup to your Google account, if you have Android backup turned on. That backup is run by Google under Google's privacy policy, and we can't see it. It can include the names and email addresses of the people in a household you're part of.

Cook mode's timers run on your phone. When one is up, Recipes shows a notification (if you allow notifications); nothing about it leaves the phone.

Signing in with Google

Signing in is optional. You need it for Premium and for sharing.

  • For Premium, Recipes asks Google only for your basic profile: your name, email address and profile picture. Signing in this way tells Recipes who you are. It doesn't give the app access to your Google Drive or anything else in your Google account.
  • For backing up My Recipe Box, sharing (households and shared cookbooks) and importing from Google Docs, Recipes also asks for permission to see and manage files in your Google Drive. It asks only when you connect Google Drive or open Import from Google Docs.
  • For Google Tasks, Recipes asks for permission to manage your tasks. It asks only when you turn on grocery list sync with Google Tasks.

You can sign out in the app at any time, and you can remove Recipes' access from your Google account at https://myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Sharing with your household

When you start a household or share a cookbook, Recipes makes a folder named "Recipes – (its name)" in your own Google Drive and keeps the shared recipes, recipe photos, meal plan, pantry, grocery list and spice levels there. Recipes in My Recipe Box aren't shared with anyone unless you put them in a shared household or cookbook.

When you invite someone, Google Drive shares that folder with their email address and Google sends them an email about it. Household members can add and change things; people you share a cookbook with can only read it. The list of members, with their email addresses and the names they choose, is kept in the folder too.

These files live in your Google Drive (and in the Drive of anyone who starts a household you join). They are not sent to us or stored on our servers. Leaving or deleting a household in the app doesn't delete the Drive folder; you can delete it in Google Drive.

My Recipe Box in your Google Drive

Once you've connected Google Drive, Recipes keeps a copy of My Recipe Box (your own recipes and their photos, your notes, meal plan, pantry and grocery list) in a folder of its own in your Google Drive, "Recipes – My Recipe Box", shared with no one, so a new phone, or the app installed again, gets it all back when you sign in. It's kept up to date as you change things. Like the shared folders, it's yours: it isn't sent to us, and you can delete it in Google Drive.

Your profile and ratings in your Google Drive

Once you've connected Google Drive, Recipes keeps a copy of your profile (the name you gave, the cuisines you like, how spicy, your diet, what's always in your kitchen and your swaps) and your recipe ratings in one file in your own Google Drive, "Recipes – My profile.json", so a new phone, or the app installed again, gets them back when you sign in. It's kept up to date as you change them. Like the shared folders, it's yours: it isn't sent to us, and you can delete it in Google Drive.

Importing from Google Docs

If you choose Import from Google Docs, Recipes lists the Google Docs and Word files in your Google Drive, and downloads the ones you tick to your phone, where it reads them into recipes. It doesn't change or delete your documents, it remembers only which ones it has imported, and nothing is sent to us.

Google Tasks and Google Keep

If you turn on Google Tasks sync, Recipes copies your grocery list into a list in your Google Tasks and keeps the two in step. If you send a list to Google Keep, Recipes hands the text to the Keep app on your phone, and Keep saves it to your Google account. Neither goes through us.

Premium: photos read for you

Premium's recipe scanning and pantry photo features send the photos you choose to read to the Recipes relay, a small service we run on Cloudflare. The relay checks that you're signed in and have Premium, then passes the photos to Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, and returns what Claude read: the recipe's text, or a list of ingredients.

  • The relay doesn't store your photos or the recipes read from them.
  • Anthropic processes the photos to give us the answer and doesn't use them to train its models. It deletes them within 30 days, unless they're flagged for breaking its usage policy, when it may keep them longer to check for misuse.
  • Photos are sent only when you tap Read recipe or choose a pantry photo.

To check that you have Premium, count free tries and keep to a daily limit, the relay keeps your email address with a count of the scans you've used. Today's count is replaced each day. The count of free tries of each Premium feature is kept so that reinstalling the app doesn't reset them.

So you aren't asked to sign in again every hour, the relay gives the app a sign-in pass that lasts 30 days. It's kept on your phone, and says only which Google account it's for.

The made-for-you meal plan runs on your phone. Recipes only checks with the relay that you have Premium, or counts a free try.

Premium: Fix it for me

When you tap Fix it for me in the recipe editor, Recipes sends that recipe's title, ingredients and steps (and which ingredients no step names, and which foods the steps name that aren't in the ingredients) to the Recipes relay, which passes them to Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, and sends back suggested changes. You see each change and choose which to use; nothing in the recipe changes until you do.

  • The relay doesn't store the recipe or the changes.
  • Anthropic processes the recipe to give us the answer and doesn't use it to train its models, and deletes it as it does photos (above).
  • A recipe is sent only when you tap Fix it for me.

To keep to a daily limit and count free tries, the relay counts how many recipes you've had checked today, with your email address, as it does scans.

Premium: recipes read aloud

When Cook mode reads a step aloud in a natural voice, Recipes sends the text of that step to the Recipes relay, which passes it to Google Cloud Text-to-Speech and sends back the audio. Recipes keeps the audio on your phone, so a step isn't sent twice.

  • The relay doesn't store the text or the audio.
  • To keep to a daily limit and count free tries, the relay keeps your email address with how many characters were read aloud today (replaced each day) and which recipes have used a free try (as a code made from the recipe, not the recipe itself).
  • Without a connection, or past the daily limit, Recipes reads with your phone's own voice instead, and nothing is sent.

Paying for Premium

Premium is a subscription you buy in Google Play. Google Play takes the payment and keeps your payment details; Recipes and the relay never see them. After you buy, the relay asks Google Play whether the purchase is real, paid for and still running, and keeps, with your email address, the purchase's reference from Google Play and when it ends, so it knows you have Premium. To show Google Play it was bought by your Google account without telling Google Play anything new, the purchase carries a scrambled code made from your email address that can't be turned back into it. You can cancel in Google Play at any time.

Hands-free cooking

Listening for commands is off unless you turn it on. When it's on, Recipes listens in Cook mode for a few words (Next, Back, Repeat, Ingredients, Done, Stop) using Android's speech recognizer. It uses the microphone only while hands-free is on with listening turned on, and stops listening while it reads aloud.

  • On phones that can recognise speech on the phone itself, the sound never leaves the phone.
  • On other phones, Android's speech service (from Google) turns what you say into words, under Google's privacy policy.
  • Recipes only receives the words, uses them to move through the recipe, and keeps no recordings and no text.

Casting to a TV or Nest Hub

When you cast Cook mode, your phone sends the recipe you're cooking (the step you're on, what it uses, and any timers running) to the TV or Nest Hub on your Wi-Fi. The Cast screen itself is a page we host on Cloudflare; it shows what your phone sends and keeps nothing. Google's Cast software sends Google anonymous information about how casting is used, to improve it; it doesn't include the recipe.

Sending a recipe to someone

When you share one of your own recipes, Recipes makes a file of it (its ingredients, steps, notes, photo and who it's by) and hands it to the app you pick, such as Quick Share, Messages or email. Sharing a recipe from a website sends only its name and link. Neither goes through us. A recipe file someone sends you is opened on your phone and saved only if you choose to.

Other things that connect to the internet

  • Importing a recipe from a link. Recipes downloads that page from the website it's on, as a browser would, to read the recipe. The website sees an ordinary request from your phone. If the site won't hand the recipe over that way, Recipes opens the page in a browser inside the app that you don't see, reads the recipe there, and closes it.
  • Browse and import. The built-in browser opens Google search; your searches go to Google and the sites you visit, as in any browser.
  • The page scanner (finding a page's edges and flattening it before a Premium scan) is Google's ML Kit document scanner, which runs on your phone. ML Kit may send Google information about how the scanner performs (such as device model, app version and error codes) to fix problems. It doesn't send your photos.
  • Send feedback opens a Google Form in your browser. Anything you type there goes to the form's owner (us) through Google Forms.

Screenshots and screen recording

On Android 14 and later the app is told when you take a screenshot of it, and on Android 15 and later when it's being screen-recorded, so it can offer to add who wrote the recipe. The app doesn't see or keep the screenshot or recording.

Our website

The Recipes website (ridgewoodapps.com) shows no ads. We use Google Analytics to see how many people visit and which pages they read. It uses cookies and collects things like the pages you view, roughly where you are (country and city), your device and browser, and the site or link that sent you; it doesn't tell us who you are. We've turned off Google's advertising features and Google signals. If you visit from the European Economic Area, the UK or Switzerland, no analytics cookies are set and visits are counted without them. You can block Google Analytics with Google's browser add-on at https://tools.google.com/dlpage/gaoptout, or by blocking cookies in your browser. The app itself has no analytics (below).

The beta sign-up and launch news forms on the website are Google Forms. They load from Google only when you open them. What you type there (your email address, which phone you use, and any other answers) goes to us through Google Forms and is kept in a Google Sheet. We use it only to add you to the beta and to email you about Recipes, and we don't share or sell it.

What we don't do

  • No ads, and no advertising ID.
  • No analytics or tracking in the app (Google's scanner diagnostics and Cast usage information, above, are the only usage information that leaves the app), and no selling or renting of your information. Google Analytics is on the website only.
  • No access to your contacts, location or camera roll. The microphone is used only while hands-free cooking is listening (above). Photos come from the camera or the photo picker, only when you choose one.

Google API Services

Recipes' use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

In plain words: Recipes uses your Google Drive and Google Tasks only to provide the backup, sharing and grocery list features you turned on. That data isn't used for ads, isn't sold, isn't used to train AI models, and isn't read by people, except with your permission, for security, or where the law requires it.

Keeping or deleting your information

  • On your phone: delete recipes and lists in the app, or uninstall Recipes to remove everything it stored on the phone.
  • In your Google Drive and Google Tasks: delete the "Recipes – …" folders (My Recipe Box's among them) and the "Recipes – My profile.json" file in Drive, and the grocery list in Tasks. They're yours and stay until you delete them.
  • Beta and launch news sign-ups: email us at the address below and we'll take you off the list and delete your answers within 30 days.
  • On the relay: in the app, Profile, Settings, Delete my Premium data deletes it at once. Or email us at the address below from the Google account you signed in with, with the subject "Delete my data", and we'll delete your email address, your scan and read-aloud counts, the record of which recipes used a free try, and the record of your Premium purchase, within 30 days. Nothing else about you is kept there. (Premium itself stays yours in Google Play: Restore in the app brings it back.)

Children

Recipes is for people 13 and older. It isn't directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect information from them.

Security

Everything Recipes sends over the internet is encrypted (HTTPS). Data on your phone is protected by your phone's own security.

Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update it here and change the effective date. If a change affects what we do with your information, we'll say so in the app.
